# Stage 2 Research Decision Table

| Decision area | Current binding constraint | Required action | Failure if ignored |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compromise prediction | One scalar did not jointly predict outbreak initiation and final compromise size | Use phase-separated or dynamic message-passing models and retain static non-backtracking as the scalar baseline | High outbreak rank discrimination can be mistaken for accurate blast-radius prediction |
| Rapid fleet admission | Cumulative reflected backlog across finite assurance resources | Instrument identity, credential, authorization, telemetry, and supervisory work; enforce a calibrated admission budget | Transient queue growth and delayed assurance processing can occur even when final fleet population is stable |
| Authorization segmentation | Attack reachability cannot be inferred from authorization topology alone | Maintain directed multilayer representations for authorization, communications, common dependencies, supervision, and identity infrastructure | Propagation pathways and common-mode compromise can be underestimated |
| 10,000-agent operation | No externally calibrated model and no full-scale adversarial validation | Complete empirical calibration, implementation-specific threat modeling, formal verification of critical components, and operational T&E | Model-conditioned findings can be misrepresented as deployment assurance |
| Research publication | No candidate met every preregistered support condition | Publish negative and mixed results as such; frame novelty around the assurance-specific experimental synthesis rather than constituent mathematics | Overclaiming weakens scientific credibility and creates priority risk |
